Detailed Implementation Report: Integrating Predictive ML and Generative AI for Enterprise Decision Support1. Executive Philosophy: The Shift from "Glued AI" to Modular Decision SystemsAs organizations scale their AI capabilities, the strategic pivot from simple "glued" LLM wrappers to compound AI systems is a mandatory evolution for maintaining competitive advantage. Modern enterprise architecture requires a separation of concerns that preserves stable, typed service boundaries while progressively adding orchestration and policy controls. This approach treats AI as a modular decision system rather than a monolithic black box, ensuring that we do not replace deterministic software with agents where simple functions are sufficient.The fundamental value of this separation is the mitigation of "automation bias" and confabulation. Predictive Machine Learning (ML) excels at estimation based on historical patterns, yet it lacks the contextual reasoning required for safe prescription. Generative AI (GenAI) provides natural language interaction and heterogeneous context synthesis but is historically unreliable for strict logical enforcement. Crucially, a GenAI recommendation is not a valid prescription until it has been validated against decision cost, utility, or regret. By isolating these layers, we ensure that GenAI contextualizes evidence without silently altering constraints or recomputing authoritative data.Distinct and Complementary RolesCapabilityBest AtWeaknessesAppropriate OutputCausal Inference / ExperimentationPredictive MLLearning repeatable relationships from historical data (forecasting, ranking).Vulnerable to drift; lacks operational explanation; correlation $\neq$ causation.Probabilities, forecasts, intervals, anomaly scores.N/A (Correlation based).Generative AISynthesizing heterogeneous context; retrieving/explaining knowledge; interaction.Confabulation risk; inconsistent reasoning; may omit constraints.Recommendation narratives, rationales, structured proposals.N/A (Synthesis based).Optimization / PolicySelecting alternatives under capacities, budgets, and constraints.Requires explicit objectives and trustworthy parameters.Feasible or optimal action sets.N/A (Optimization based).Rules / ControlsEnforcing permissions, thresholds, and safety invariants.Brittle for ambiguous or novel situations.Allow, deny, escalate, or constrain.N/A (Deterministic).Causal InferenceEstimating what an intervention changes rather than merely what will happen.Requires identification assumptions or carefully designed analysis.Treatment effects; evidence of influence.Primary Strength.Transitioning from this philosophy to practice requires a phased roadmap to manage technical debt and ensure that technical capabilities align with operational risks.2. We introduce high-complexity components—such as durable workflow engines or agent frameworks—only when the scale of the decision logic justifies the overhead. The Reference Architecture: Predict → Prescribe → Decide → LearnTo ensure GenAI recommendations are grounded in authoritative data, we insist on a "closed-loop" architecture governed by a Mandatory Prediction Contract. This contract is the only way to prevent GenAI from silently altering constraints, rounding values into different decision categories, or reinterpreting frozen statistical evidence. The LLM receives a read-only serialization and is strictly forbidden from recalculating prediction fields.Major Integration PatternsPrediction-enriched Assistant (ML → GenAI): GenAI explains a risk score and proposes steps. Caution: Do not let the LLM invent drivers, confidence levels, or causal effects.Generate then Rank (GenAI → ML): GenAI creates candidates; a classifier scores them for quality. Caution: The ranker must be trained for the actual selection objective, not general relevance.Predict-Optimize-Explain (ML → Optimizer → GenAI): An optimizer selects the best route; GenAI explains the rationale. Caution: GenAI must not silently alter constraints within the explanation.Closed-loop Policy (Prediction → Action → Outcome): Outcomes feed retraining. Caution: Rigorous control for selection bias and delayed outcomes is mandatory.The Prediction Contract (JSON Schema){

Production-Ready Technical Implementation and Folder HierarchyA "Flow-first" application design is paramount to isolate business logic from agent reasoning. The Flow owns the deterministic sequencing and state, while the Crew handles the GenAI analysis, preventing "excessive agency" where agents might attempt to bypass security or authorization boundaries.Recommended Full Folder Treeconfigs/: Environment-specific thresholds and provider selections.data/: Raw training data and the MVP feedback store (feedback.jsonl).knowledge/: Curated policies and runbooks (Source root for RAG).src/project_finance_ai/:adapters/: Replaceable provider integrations (LLM factories, persistence).contracts/: Shared typed boundaries (prediction.py, recommendation.py).ml/: PREDICT layer (Predictor, features, registry); no agent logic here.services/: Domain services (Knowledge, Policy, Feedback).orchestration/:flows/: Durable state management (Predict → Prescribe → Decide → Learn).crews/: GenAI agents (Context Analyst and Recommendation Writer).tools/custom/: BaseTool adapters for internal services.guardrails/: Deterministic controls for schema validity and approval policy.tests/: Contract, unit, and end-to-end human-review cycle tests.Crew and Task OwnershipThe system utilizes two primary agents with specific implementation-driven tool access:Context Analyst Agent: Interprets the situation around the ML prediction.Tools (Deterministic Python/RAG): PredictionContextTool (read-only ML result), KnowledgeRetrievalTool (runbook passages), PolicyLookupTool (thresholds/rules), EvidenceAnalysisTool (organizes findings).Recommendation Writer Agent: Converts evidence into a clear action proposal.Tools (Generative Reasoning): RecommendationTool (proposes action), RecommendationExplanationTool (drafts rationale).This hierarchy ensures that reasoning agents act upon immutable evidence gathered by deterministic tools.5. Governance, Safety, and Dual-System EvaluationIndependent evaluation of predictive and prescriptive components is required to ensure end-to-end reliability. A "Validator/Critic Loop" ensures every GenAI proposal is checked by deterministic guardrails, but we must use stopping conditions to avoid endless or self-reinforcing loops.Evaluation ChecklistsPrediction Quality[ ] Discrimination: ROC-AUC and Precision-Recall targets met.[ ] Calibration: Brier score and calibration curves verified.[ ] Operational: Latency, feature freshness, and Abstention rates.[ ] Stability: Checked for feature drift and Training-serving skew.Prescription Quality[ ] Groundedness: All claims cited to supplied context/provenance.[ ] Policy Compliance: Action types within the defined allowlist.[ ] Utility: Measured by decision cost/regret vs. human benchmarks.[ ] Safety: Human override rate and safety-violation rate (Target: zero).Principal Risks and MitigationsRiskMitigationConfabulationRAG with provenance; mandatory citations; structured output schemas.Excessive AgencyLeast-privilege tools; downstream authorization; human approval gates.Automation BiasExplicitly surface uncertainty; show intervals rather than point scores.Distribution DriftContinuous monitoring of features and delayed ground-truth performance.7.
